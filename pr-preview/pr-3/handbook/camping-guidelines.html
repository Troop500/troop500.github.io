Camping Guidelines

Camping Guidelines

Troop 500G camps monthly, providing scouts with regular outdoor adventure opportunities. Our camping program is designed to build outdoor skills, foster independence, and create lasting memories.

Monthly Outings

Frequency: Most outings are Friday evening through Sunday mid-day
Purpose: Complete rank requirements, earn merit badges, and enjoy outdoor activities
Activities: Camping, hiking, cooking, outdoor skills training, and team building

Camping Organization

Patrol System

  • Scouts camp and cook in patrols
  • Each patrol typically has a “Grubmaster” responsible for meal planning and preparation
  • Patrols work together to set up camp and complete activities

Tent Partners

  • Scouts most often tent with a buddy
  • Tent partners can be no more than 2 years apart in age
  • All tents are supplied by the troop

What the Troop Provides

  • Tents and camping equipment
  • Cooking equipment and supplies
  • Basic camping gear (stoves, patrol boxes, etc.)

What Scouts Must Bring

Personal Items Required:

  • All personal clothes and toiletries
  • Sleeping bag and sleeping pad
  • Camp chair
  • Mess kit (fork/spoon/knife, plate, cup in mesh bag)
  • Day backpack with individual first aid kit
  • Scout handbook
  • Ten Essentials

Recommended Packing Lists:

  • Tent Camping Checklist
  • Additional lists available on the Resources page

Registration and Fees

RSVP Process:

  • RSVPs completed through Scoutbook
  • Google Form completion required (link provided in Scoutbook)
  • Indicate commitment to paying scout’s fees for the event

Fee Structure:

  • Fees vary per outing and are paid in advance
  • Fees are based on attendance numbers for planning purposes
  • Payment covers registration, campsite reservations, and food costs

Important: We often register scouts in advance for Council events and reserve campsites based on RSVP numbers, so accurate responses are essential.

Safety Requirements

Two-Deep Leadership

  • At least two adults present for any scouting activity
  • Additional adult participation often needed for outings

Medical Requirements

  • Current medical forms required (Parts A & B minimum)
  • Part C required for summer camp and high adventure
  • Medical forms available here

Youth Protection

  • All scouts and adults trained in Youth Protection
  • Buddy system used for all activities
  • Safe Scouting guidelines followed

Camping Skills Development

Progressive Skill Building

Camping provides opportunities to develop:

  • Outdoor cooking and meal planning
  • Tent setup and campsite organization
  • Navigation and hiking skills
  • Weather awareness and preparation
  • Leave No Trace principles

Duty Rosters

Each patrol uses duty rosters to organize camp responsibilities:

  • Water Master
  • Fire Master
  • Head Cook (usually Grubmaster)
  • Assistant Cook
  • Campsite Cleanup
  • Kitchen Cleanup

Special Camping Programs

Summer Camp

  • Week-long resident camp experience
  • Merit badge opportunities
  • Advanced outdoor skills training
  • Swimming and waterfront activities

High Adventure

  • Available to older, experienced scouts
  • Extended backpacking or specialty activities
  • Higher physical and skill requirements
  • Additional training and preparation required

Weather Considerations

Year-Round Camping

  • Troop camps in all seasons and weather conditions
  • Proper gear selection critical for comfort and safety
  • Weather-appropriate clothing and equipment lists provided

Cancellation Policy

  • Severe weather may result in outing modifications or cancellations
  • Safety always takes precedence over scheduled activities

Equipment Care

Personal Responsibility

  • Scouts responsible for their personal gear
  • Pack and organize gear efficiently
  • Care for borrowed troop equipment

Troop Gear Maintenance

  • Tent maintenance procedures
  • Proper cleaning and storage required
  • Report damaged equipment promptly
