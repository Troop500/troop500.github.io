Safety & Youth Protection

Safety & Youth Protection Policies

Scouting promises adventure, learning, challenge, and responsibility. To fulfill these promises safely, Troop 500G maintains comprehensive safety policies and procedures.

Core Safety Principles

Safe Scouting Foundation

  • Adventure and learning must be conducted safely
  • All activities follow Scouting America safety guidelines
  • Prevention and preparation are key to safe activities
  • Every scout and adult shares responsibility for safety

Youth Protection Training

Required Training

  • All scout leaders: Trained and certified in Youth Protection program
  • All scouts and parents: Must complete Youth Protection exercise
  • Documentation: Submit completed exercises to troop leadership

Training Components

  • Recognition of inappropriate behavior
  • Prevention strategies and awareness
  • Intervention procedures when problems arise
  • Reporting requirements and procedures

Two-Deep Leadership

Adult Supervision Requirements

  • Minimum requirement: At least two adults present for ANY scouting activity
  • Critical for: Outings, summer camp, high adventure activities
  • Parent participation: Essential to achieve required supervision levels

Why Two-Deep Leadership Matters

  • Ensures adult accountability and oversight
  • Provides support for youth leaders
  • Creates safer environment for all participants
  • Meets Scouting America mandatory requirements

Communication Safety

Technology Guidelines

  • GroupMe communication: Whole group chat for adults and scouts
  • Scout-specific chat: Monitored by two-deep adult leadership
  • One-on-one rule: Youth should NEVER communicate with adults 1-on-1
  • Virtual communication: Same two-deep requirements as in-person

Phone and Device Policies

  • Scouts may bring phones to meetings and outings
  • Use technology appropriately during activities
  • Problematic use addressed case-by-case
  • Technology doesn’t replace face-to-face interaction

Physical Safety

Camping and Outdoor Activities

  • Follow Leave No Trace principles
  • Use buddy system for all activities
  • Maintain proper hydration and nutrition
  • Appropriate clothing and gear for conditions

Skills-Based Safety

  • Knife safety: Totin’ Chip training required
  • Fire safety: Firem’n Chit training required
  • First aid: Basic first aid skills taught
  • Emergency procedures: Regular training and practice

Bullying Prevention

Safe Environment Commitment

Safe scouting includes protection from:

  • Physical abuse or threats
  • Sexual abuse or inappropriate behavior
  • Emotional abuse and bullying
  • Harassment or discrimination

Recognition and Response

  • Train all members to recognize inappropriate behavior
  • Provide clear reporting procedures
  • Address concerns promptly and appropriately
  • Create supportive environment for all scouts

Medical Safety

Required Medical Documentation

  • Parts A & B: Required for all events
  • Part C: Required for summer camp and high adventure
  • Current forms: Must be up-to-date for participation
  • Emergency contacts: Accessible to leaders during activities

Health and Safety Procedures

  • First aid training for adult leaders
  • Emergency action plans for all activities
  • Access to medical care during outings
  • Medication management for scouts with special needs

Emergency Procedures

Emergency Contacts

  • Adult leaders maintain current emergency contact information
  • Medical information available during all activities
  • Communication plan with parents during emergencies
  • Coordination with local emergency services when needed

Incident Reporting

  • Document all incidents and injuries
  • Report serious incidents to council and parents immediately
  • Follow up on all safety concerns
  • Learn from incidents to prevent future problems

Safe Scouting Resources

Guide to Safe Scouting

  • Available in front of every Scout Handbook
  • Also available in troop Google Drive folder
  • Review with your scout regularly
  • Covers comprehensive safety topics

Personal Safety Videos

Watch age-appropriate videos: https://www.scouting.org/training/youth/scouts-bsa/

  • Videos correspond to current rank level
  • Cover various safety topics
  • Required viewing for all scouts
  • Discuss content with parents

Creating a Safe Environment

Everyone’s Responsibility

  • Adults model appropriate behavior
  • Scouts support and protect each other
  • Parents stay informed and involved
  • Open communication encouraged

Reporting Concerns

  • Any safety concerns should be reported immediately
  • Multiple reporting channels available
  • Confidential reporting options provided
  • No retaliation for good-faith reporting

Additional Safety Training

Ongoing Education

  • Regular safety topics at troop meetings
  • Hands-on safety skills training
  • Emergency response drills
  • Safety awareness during all activities

Leadership Development

  • Youth leaders trained in safety responsibilities
  • Adult leaders receive comprehensive safety training
  • Regular updates on safety policies and procedures
  • Continuous improvement in safety practices

Safety is everyone’s responsibility. When in doubt, ask questions and seek guidance.
