Adult Leadership Philosophy

Youth-Led, Adult-Supported

  • Youth provide leadership and make decisions
  • Adults guide, mentor, and ensure safety
  • Adults facilitate rather than direct
  • Youth learn through experience and responsibility

Two-Deep Leadership

  • Minimum two adults present at all activities
  • Ensures safety and proper supervision
  • Provides mentorship and support
  • Required by Scouting America policy

Additional Leadership Opportunities

Available Positions

Other adult leadership roles available within the troop:

  • Assistant Scoutmaster positions
  • Merit badge counselors
  • Committee members
  • Activity coordinators
  • Advancement coordinators

Getting Involved

  • All new adults encouraged to consider leadership roles
  • Training provided for all positions
  • “All hands on deck” approach works best
  • Various time commitments available

Adult Training Requirements

Required Training

  • Youth Protection Training: Mandatory for all adult leaders
  • Position-specific training: Required for each leadership role
  • Continuing education: Ongoing training opportunities

Training Benefits

  • Better support for youth development
  • Understanding of scouting methods and goals
  • Safety awareness and procedures
  • Leadership skill development

Adult Responsibilities

Program Support

  • Facilitate troop meetings and activities
  • Provide transportation and supervision for outings
  • Support merit badge and advancement programs
  • Maintain equipment and facilities

Safety and Supervision

  • Ensure safe environment for all activities
  • Implement youth protection policies
  • Provide medical response when needed
  • Monitor youth behavior and development

Mentorship

  • Guide youth leaders in their roles
  • Provide advice and support when requested
  • Model appropriate behavior and values
  • Foster positive character development

Committee Structure

Troop Committee Functions

  • Policy development and oversight
  • Financial management and budgeting
  • Adult recruitment and training
  • Charter organization relationships

Committee Meetings

  • Regular meetings to discuss troop business
  • Planning for major activities and events
  • Review of policies and procedures
  • Support for Scoutmaster and youth leaders

Parent Involvement

Expectations

  • Active participation in troop operations
  • Support for daughters’ scouting experience
  • Volunteer for activities and events
  • Maintain open communication with leaders

Volunteer Opportunities

  • Outings and camping trips
  • Merit badge counseling
  • Transportation assistance
  • Special event coordination
  • Administrative support

Leadership Development

For Adults

  • Ongoing training opportunities
  • Leadership skill development
  • Networking with other scouting leaders
  • Personal growth through service

Supporting Youth Leadership

  • Allow youth to lead and learn from mistakes
  • Provide guidance without taking over
  • Recognize and celebrate youth achievements
  • Foster independence and responsibility

Communication

With Youth

  • Maintain appropriate boundaries
  • Follow two-deep leadership requirements
  • Encourage youth decision-making
  • Provide positive reinforcement

With Parents

  • Regular updates on troop activities
  • Open communication about concerns
  • Transparency in decision-making
  • Collaborative approach to youth development

Getting Started as an Adult Leader

First Steps

  1. Complete Youth Protection Training
  2. Observe troop meetings and activities
  3. Talk with current leaders about opportunities
  4. Consider your interests and available time
  5. Complete position-specific training

Ongoing Support

  • Mentorship from experienced leaders
  • Access to training resources
  • Support from council and district
  • Connection with other troop leaders

Adult leadership is essential to the success of our troop. We welcome all adults who want to support our scouts’ development and adventures.
