# Getting Involved - Parents

While Scouting America is youth-led, it requires dedicated adult volunteers who undergo comprehensive training in youth protection, position-specific skills, advanced leadership development, and event/safety-specific training. Parent involvement at whatever level can be supported by your family is essential to the success of our troop. There's an opportunity for every parent, whether you're a previous Eagle Scout or have never heard of Scouting before last week.

## Supporting Troop Events and Activities

The simplest way to start getting involved is by providing minimal support for troop events and activities. These contributions are invaluable and help our troop function smoothly:

**Transportation Support**
- Drive Scouts to and from meetings and events
- Assist with carpooling arrangements for outings
- Help transport equipment to activity locations

**Event Assistance**
- Help with setup and cleanup at troop activities
- Assist with simple tasks during meetings or outings
- Support special events like courts of honor or fundraisers

**Equipment Maintenance**
- Help clean and restock troop equipment during semi-annual planning conferences
- Assist with basic gear maintenance and organization
- Help acquire new gear donated from various sources
- Support inventory and equipment management efforts

These activities typically require Youth Protection Training and provide a great way to see how the troop operates while making meaningful contributions.

## Becoming a Registered Leader

The next step is becoming a registered adult leader without a specific position. As a trained and registered adult leader, you can:

- Fill in on an as-needed basis for schedule conflicts or unforeseen circumstances
- Provide backup support for meetings and outings
- Gain experience with different aspects of troop operations
- Serve as a flexible resource for troop leadership

This role provides excellent preparation for taking on more formal leadership positions and allows you to contribute meaningfully while maintaining schedule flexibility.

## Formal Leadership Positions

### Committee Member
- Support troop operations through committee work (fundraising, advancement, activities, etc.)
- Attend monthly committee meetings
- Help with planning and coordination behind the scenes
- Various specialties available based on your interests and skills

### Assistant Scoutmaster
- Work directly with youth leaders and patrols
- Attend regular troop meetings and outings
- Mentor youth in leadership development and outdoor skills
- Requires more comprehensive training including outdoor leader skills

### Merit Badge Counselor
- Share your expertise by counseling Scouts in merit badges related to your profession or hobbies
- Flexible time commitment - work with individual Scouts or small groups
- Great way to contribute specialized knowledge

### Scoutmaster
- Primary youth program leader
- Responsible for overall program quality and youth development
- Requires extensive training and significant time commitment
- Mentors and guides all youth leaders in the troop

## Adult Leader Training

Adult leader training is available both online, in-person, and through University of Scouting events.

### Three Steps to Becoming a Trained Leader

**STEP ONE: SET UP A MY.SCOUTING ACCOUNT**
Visit [My.Scouting](https://my.scouting.org) and create an account. Once you have an account you can access online training.

**STEP TWO: TAKE YOUTH PROTECTION TRAINING**
Scouting America places the greatest importance on creating the most secure environment possible for our youth members. Youth Protection Training is required for ALL adult leaders before they can participate in any Scouting activities and must be renewed every three years.

**STEP THREE: POSITION SPECIFIC TRAINING**
Scouting America's Learning Center is at [My.Scouting](https://my.scouting.org). Select your position and a learning plan will be created for you. The plan includes modules that are designed to allow you to complete them at your own pace.

### Advanced Training Opportunities

**Wood Badge**
Wood Badge is known as the premier training course in Scouting America. Offered by a local council, it is held over a period of a week or two full weekends and includes an outdoor camping experience. Those who complete Wood Badge are presented iconic wooden beads as a symbol of their commitment to provide a quality Scouting program.

More information: [https://www.scouting.org/programs/cub-scouts/adult-leader-training/](https://www.scouting.org/programs/cub-scouts/adult-leader-training/)

### Continual Training

**Roundtable**
Once a month Scouting volunteers in a local community come together to provide information about local Scouting events, activities, and programs. Roundtables play a significant role in providing continuous supplemental training for unit leaders. This monthly event provides program topic guidance and structure suggestions for unit leaders. A fun environment creates excitement around the coming month of activities and program for unit leaders to carry back for implementation in their units.

As an alternative or in addition to in-person Roundtables you can participate in virtual Roundtables.

**University of Scouting / Pow Wow**
These opportunities may be available through your local council. Typically these are a series of courses that take place in one day and often provide the opportunity to take several sessions on various topics that help you provide a great program.

## Getting Involved - Next Steps

**Start Small**
- Complete Youth Protection Training
- Fill out the family talent survey
- Attend a parent meeting or troop committee meeting
- Volunteer for transportation or simple event tasks

**Explore Opportunities**
- Help with equipment maintenance during planning conferences
- Talk with current leaders about their experiences
- Shadow a leader at a meeting or outing
- Consider what level of involvement works for your schedule

**Make a Commitment**
- Become a registered leader for flexibility and backup support
- Choose a formal role that matches your availability and interests
- Complete required training for your chosen position
- Connect with other parents in similar roles

**Remember**: Every youth deserves a trained leader. Your commitment to training ensures we provide the highest quality Scouting program possible, and there's a place for every parent to contribute meaningfully to their Scout's experience.
