# Getting Involved - Parents

While Scouting America is youth-led, it requires dedicated adult volunteers who undergo comprehensive training in youth protection, position-specific skills, advanced leadership development, and event/safety-specific training. We encourage any and all parents to become trained leaders, but we also realize that not everyone has the capacity in their lives at this time to take on formal leadership roles.

## Support Your Scout

The best way to get involved is by helping your Scout do their best in the Scouting program. Remember, help and support does not mean do it for them. A key tenet of Scouting is to develop independent and capable leaders. As Lord Baden-Powell said, "Never do for a Scout what they can do for themselves."

Every Scout develops on their own timeline, and the level of support needed varies from Scout to Scout and changes over time. Some Scouts may need more guidance with organization and reminders, while others quickly become self-directed. What works for one Scout may not work for another, and that's completely normal! Talk to Scout leaders and other parents to learn more about everyone's Scouting journey - their experiences can provide valuable insights into supporting your own Scout's growth and development.

Here are ways to support your Scout's success:
- Remind them to follow communications and schedules by reviewing Scoutbook and troop announcements together
- Assist with getting their uniform together and ensuring they have required gear
- Encourage them to take ownership of their advancement and merit badge progress
- Support their leadership development by letting them solve problems and make decisions
- Communicate with adult leaders about accessibility options or special considerations that help our leaders better understand how to work with and support your Scout along their Scouting journey
- Be available for transportation when they need to attend meetings and activities
- Help with setup and cleanup at events while letting your Scout take the lead on their responsibilities

**Advocate for Your Scout's Needs**
Our troop provides an accessible and inclusive environment for every eligible youth. We will always do our best to support your scouts needs, but you can help our best be better. We require a joining meeting prior to your Scout's first overnight outing, but that's not the end of our discussion. You are encouraged to request a pre-event check-in with leaders attending any event (particularly helpful for summer camp) to ensure we can provide the best possible support for your Scout's success. 

## Learn About the Troop

There's a lot to learn about Scouting in general and our troop specifically! Whether you're completely new to Scouting or earned your Eagle Scout rank back in the day, the program is always evolving. New families need time to understand how everything works, and even experienced Scouting parents find that each troop has its own culture and way of doing things. We encourage you to take time to observe and learn before jumping into formal roles.

**Attending Weekly Meetings**
Parents are welcome to attend weekly meetings to learn about the troop! During meetings, Scouts and Scoutmasters will break away for dedicated skills instruction and Scout-led activities. While parents are ALWAYS welcome to observe, remember that Scouting is Scout-led and we encourage you to give your Scout room to learn and grow independently. Sometimes that means we'll let mistakes happen and allow interactions to play out naturally - our job is to create a safe and inclusive environment where Scouts can grow and learn from their experiences.

Please note that any direct contact volunteer leaders (those working directly with Scouts) are required to have completed Safeguarding Youth training before participating in Scouting activities.

**What Parents Can Do During Troop Meetings:**
- Get to know the committee members and other parents, help us build our troop community 
- Learn about upcoming events
- Take on "service in a snap" tasks that help the troop function
- Complete online training modules on your phone or device
- Go on a grocery run!  Life's busy and you don't have to attend every meeting unless you want to. 

**Parent Meetings and Committee Meetings**
- Attend monthly parent meetings to stay informed about troop activities and decisions
- Join committee meetings to participate in planning and troop business
- These meetings are great opportunities to ask questions and connect with other parents and leaders

## Support Your Troop

**How can I help?**

As volunteer adult leaders, we try to ask for help when we need it, but honestly, sometimes we don't get around to it or forget to mention it. If you have a little time and want to pitch in, we'd love to have you help! Come to our committee meetings, show up to parent meetings, or just ask any committee member how you can help. There's always more work than hands to do it, and any help is genuinely appreciated.

### Supporting Troop Events and Activities

The simplest way to start getting involved is by providing minimal support for troop events and activities. These contributions are invaluable and help our troop function smoothly:

- **Transportation**: Drive Scouts to meetings/events, assist with carpooling, and help transport equipment
- **Event Support**: Help with setup/cleanup at activities, assist with simple tasks, and support special events
- **Equipment Care**: Help maintain and organize troop gear, assist with inventory, and support equipment management

These activities typically require Safeguarding Youth Training and provide a great way to see how the troop operates while making meaningful contributions.

## Take Some Training

### Safeguarding Youth Training
Safeguarding Youth training is required for all registered adult leaders and strongly encouraged for all parents and guardians. This training helps new members understand the requirements behind every decision we make in Scouting America. Available online at [my.scouting.org](https://my.scouting.org), this training covers child abuse prevention and safe Scouting practices and must be renewed every three years.

### Online Training

My.Scouting.org offers various online training modules that can help you better understand Scouting and support your Scout's experience. These courses are available at your own pace and can help you feel more confident when participating in troop activities.

### In-Person Training
See below for details on in-person and advanced training offered through Lake Erie Council and Scouting America!  

## Adult Leadership Opportunities

### What It Means to Be an Adult Leader in Troop 500

As an adult leader in our troop, you become part of the "Association with Adults" method - one of Scouting's most powerful influences in youth development. In Troop 500, this means serving as a mentor, guide, and positive role model while supporting our Scout-led program.

Our adult leaders create safe, inclusive environments where Scouts can learn, grow, and lead. Whether you're teaching outdoor skills, mentoring a Scout through advancement, or handling troop business behind the scenes, your role is to guide rather than direct - helping Scouts develop confidence and leadership skills by allowing them to make decisions, solve problems, and learn from their experiences.

Every adult leader in our troop commits to Scouting's ideals and helps prepare our Scouts to become ethical leaders and engaged citizens in their communities.

**Complete an Adult Leader Application**
To become a registered adult leader, you'll need to complete an adult leader application through [BeAScout.scouting.org](https://beascout.scouting.org/). This site will walk you through the registration process and help you get started with your leadership journey.

**Exploring Your Options**
Before committing to a specific role:
- Talk with current leaders about their experiences
- Shadow a leader at a meeting or outing  
- Consider what level of involvement works for your schedule
- Connect with other parents in similar roles

**Becoming Position Trained**

### Registered Adult Leader
- Registered adult leader who assists as needed
- Provides backup support for outings and activities
- Flexible involvement level based on your availability
- Good stepping stone to more involved leadership roles

### Merit Badge Counselor
A merit badge counselor is a registered adult leader with knowledge and enthusiasm about a career, hobby or other subject who is willing to share that knowledge and enthusiasm with Scouts. As a merit badge counselor, you can:
- Share your expertise by counseling Scouts in merit badges related to your profession or hobbies
- Requires Safeguarding Youth Training and position-specific training
- Flexible time commitment - work with individual Scouts or small groups
- Great way to contribute without ongoing meeting attendance requirements

More information: [Merit Badge Counselor Guide](https://www.scouting.org/merit-badge-tips-guide/merit-badge-counselor/)  
Find merit badge opportunities: [Lake Erie Council Merit Badge Counselor List](https://lecscouting.org/merit-badges/)

### Committee Member
The Troop Committee handles the business side of Scouting, allowing our Scoutmasters to focus on the youth program. Committee members typically commit to:
- Attend monthly committee meetings (usually 1-2 hours, held on a weeknight)
- Support one or more specialized areas based on your interests and skills
- Help with planning and coordination behind the scenes
- Assist with troop business and administrative functions

**Committee Specialties Include:**

- **Secretary**: Keep meeting minutes and maintain troop records
- **Treasurer**: Manage troop finances and track Scout accounts
- **Outdoor Activities Coordinator**: Plan and coordinate troop outings and special events  
- **Advancement Coordinator**: Track Scout progress and coordinate boards of review
- **Chaplain**: Provides spiratual tone for troop meeting and activities
- **Training Coordinator**: Help adults complete required training
- **Equipment Coordinator**: Maintain and organize troop gear
- **Membership Coordinator**: Develop a plan for year-round membership flow into the troop
- **Others as needed**:  Troops are encouraged to engage all parents in a role that suits their skills and interests.

### Assistant Scoutmaster
- Work directly with youth leaders and patrols
- Attend regular troop meetings and outings
- Mentor youth in leadership development and outdoor skills
- Requires more comprehensive training including outdoor leader skills

### Scoutmaster
- Primary youth program leader
- Responsible for overall program quality and youth development
- Requires extensive training and significant time commitment
- Mentors and guides all youth leaders in the troop

## Adult Training Requirements

<img src="/assets/images/handbook/Trained_patch_green.jpg" alt="Scouting America Trained Patch" style="width: 150px; height: auto; float: right; margin: 0 0 15px 15px;">

Adult leaders in Scouting America must complete required training to ensure the safety and quality of the program. Our troop is committed to having well-trained leaders who can provide the best possible experience for our Scouts.

### Required Training for All Adult Leaders

**Safeguarding Youth Training**
- Required for ALL adult leaders before they can participate in any Scouting activities
- Must be renewed every three years
- Online training available at [my.scouting.org](https://my.scouting.org)
- Covers child abuse prevention and safe Scouting practices

**Position-Specific Training**
Each adult leadership position has specific training requirements:
- **Scoutmaster**: Scoutmaster Position-Specific Training
- **Assistant Scoutmaster**: Assistant Scoutmaster Position-Specific Training  
- **Committee Chair**: Committee Chair Position-Specific Training
- **Committee Members**: Committee Member Position-Specific Training
- **Merit Badge Counselor**: Merit Badge Counselor Training

### Additional Training

**Outdoor Training**
- Hazardous Weather Training - This module will cover hazardous weather conditions that Scouts need to plan ahead and steps to take to be prepared.
- Introduction to Outdoor Leader Skills (IOLS) - Working as patrols, this hands-on course provides adult leaders the practical outdoor skills they need to lead Scouts in the out-of-doors. Upon completion, leaders should feel comfortable teaching Scouts the basic skills required to obtain the First Class rank. Along with Scoutmaster Specifics this course is required of all direct contact leaders registered in Scouts BSA Troops in order to be considered "trained". ([IOLS Information](https://filestore.scouting.org/filestore/training/pdf/IOLS_33640_2016.pdf))
- Wilderness First Aid - Recommended for outdoor activity leaders
- Leave No Trace Trainer - Environmental stewardship training

**Leadership Development**
- Wood Badge - Advanced leadership training for new and experienced leaders ([Wood Badge Information](https://www.scouting.org/training/resources-for-program-trainers-training-committees/woodbadge/))
- University of Scouting - Council-sponsored training events
- Scouting EDGE - The EDGE method is practiced throughout Scouting, and is Scouting's most effective training tool. Whether training Scout leaders in their position or teaching new outdoor skills to youth or new adult leaders, this training will give you the tools to keep your audience engaged while helping your instruction stick in their minds. 

**Event and Safety-Specific Training**
- Climb On Safely - For climbing and rappelling activities
- Safety Afloat - For aquatic activities
- Trek Safely - For hiking and backpacking
- Shooting Sports Director Training - For firearms and archery programs

**Getting Started with Training**
1. Complete Safeguarding Youth Training (your first priority)
2. Register for Position-Specific Training (check with the Training Coordinator)
3. Plan for Outdoor Training if you'll be involved in outdoor activities
4. Consider Advanced Training like Wood Badge for leadership development

For training questions or assistance with registration, contact our Training Coordinator or visit the [Lake Erie Council website](https://lecscouting.org/).

### Continual Training

**Roundtable**
Once a month Scouting volunteers in a local community come together to provide information about local Scouting events, activities, and programs. Roundtables play a significant role in providing continuous supplemental training for unit leaders. This monthly event provides program topic guidance and structure suggestions for unit leaders. A fun environment creates excitement around the coming month of activities and program for unit leaders to carry back for implementation in their units. As an alternative or in addition to in-person Roundtables you can participate in virtual Roundtables.

**University of Scouting / Pow Wow**
These opportunities may be available through your local council. Typically these are a series of courses that take place in one day and often provide the opportunity to take several sessions on various topics that help you provide a great program.

**Remember**: Every youth deserves a trained leader. Your commitment to training ensures we provide the highest quality Scouting program possible, and there's a place for every parent to contribute meaningfully to their Scout's experience.
