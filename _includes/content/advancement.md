# Advancement

Advancement in Scouting America provides a progressive series of challenges that help Scouts develop skills, confidence, and leadership abilities.

## Rank Advancement

Scouts advance through the following ranks:
1. **Scout** - Entry level rank
2. **Tenderfoot** - Basic outdoor and citizenship skills
3. **Second Class** - Expanded outdoor skills and community service
4. **First Class** - Advanced outdoor skills and leadership
5. **Star** - Leadership development and merit badges
6. **Life** - Continued leadership and merit badge work
7. **Eagle** - The highest rank in Scouting, requiring an Eagle project

## Merit Badges

Merit badges allow Scouts to explore interests and develop skills in specific areas. Scouts can earn merit badges through:
- Troop meetings and activities
- Merit badge workshops at camps
- Individual study with merit badge counselors
- Community programs and classes

Popular merit badges include Camping, Hiking, First Aid, Cooking, and many others covering topics from arts to STEM to outdoor skills.

## Board of Review

Each rank advancement requires a board of review, where Scouts discuss their experiences and demonstrate their understanding of Scouting principles with adult leaders.

## Eagle Scout Project

The Eagle Scout project is a significant leadership service project that demonstrates a Scout's ability to plan, lead, and execute a project that benefits their community or an organization other than Scouting America.
