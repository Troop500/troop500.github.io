# Packing Lists & Gear

Proper packing and gear selection are essential for safe, comfortable, and successful outdoor adventures. This section provides comprehensive packing guidance for various activities.

## The Ten Essentials

Any time a scout goes camping or hiking, she should always carry these essential items for emergency preparedness and outdoor safety.

### Essential Items Checklist
- [ ] **Pocketknife** (when you complete Totin' Chip training)
- [ ] **First Aid Kit** (personal kit with basic supplies)
- [ ] **Extra clothing** (appropriate for weather conditions)
- [ ] **Rain gear** (poncho or rain jacket and pants)
- [ ] **Filled water bottle** (minimum 1 liter capacity)
- [ ] **Flashlight or headlamp** (with extra batteries)
- [ ] **Trail food** (energy bars, nuts, dried fruit)
- [ ] **Matches/fire starter** (when you complete Firem'n Chit training)
- [ ] **Sun protection** (hat, sunscreen, sunglasses)
- [ ] **Map and compass** (appropriate for activity area)

### Recommended Additional Items
- [ ] **Insect repellant** (appropriate for season and location)
- [ ] **Safety whistle** (for emergency signaling)
- [ ] **Toilet paper** (small roll in plastic bag with hand sanitizer)

## Tent Camping Checklist

For typical weekend tent camping trips, use this comprehensive packing list.

### Essential Gear
- [ ] **[Ten Essentials](#the-ten-essentials)** (all items listed above)
- [ ] **Sleeping bag** (appropriate temperature rating)
- [ ] **Sleeping pad** (for insulation and comfort)
- [ ] **Pillow** (optional - can use inflatable or stuff sack)

### Clothing
- [ ] **Scout uniform** (wear to and from campsite)
- [ ] **Weather-appropriate clothes** (layers recommended)
- [ ] **Pajamas/sleepwear**
- [ ] **Extra underwear and socks**
- [ ] **Boots or hiking shoes**
- [ ] **Camp shoes** (comfortable shoes for around camp)

### Personal Items
- [ ] **Toiletries** (toothbrush, toothpaste, soap, etc.)
- [ ] **Towel** (quick-dry camping towel preferred)
- [ ] **Personal medications** (in original containers)

### Dining and Activities
- [ ] **Mess kit** (fork/spoon/knife, plate, cup in mesh bag)
- [ ] **Scout handbook** (for advancement and reference)
- [ ] **Small backpack/daypack** (for day activities)
- [ ] **Books for reading** (entertainment during downtime)

### Practical Items
- [ ] **Trash bag** (for personal trash and wet items)
- [ ] **Plastic bags** (for organizing and waterproofing)
- [ ] **Duct tape** (small roll for repairs)

## Seasonal Considerations

### Summer Camping
**Additional items for hot weather:**
- Extra water bottles or hydration system
- Lightweight, breathable clothing
- Sun hat with wide brim
- Higher SPF sunscreen
- Cooling towel
- Insect repellant (especially important)

### Winter Camping
**Additional items for cold weather:**
- Warmer sleeping bag (rated for expected temperatures)
- Insulated sleeping pad
- Thermal underwear and layers
- Warm hat and gloves
- Waterproof boots
- Hand and foot warmers
- Extra high-energy food

### Rainy Season
**Additional items for wet conditions:**
- Extra rain gear and waterproof layers
- Waterproof bag for electronics
- Extra socks and underwear
- Quick-dry clothing materials
- Waterproof matches or lighter
- Tarp for additional shelter

## Personal Gear vs. Troop Equipment

### Provided by Troop
- Tents and rain flies
- Cooking equipment (stoves, pots, utensils)
- Patrol boxes with basic supplies
- First aid kits (patrol-level)
- Water containers (large patrol containers)

### Personal Responsibility
- All clothing and personal items
- Individual sleeping systems
- Personal first aid supplies
- Individual water bottles
- Personal tools and equipment
- Entertainment and reading materials

## Packing Tips

### Organization Strategies
- **Use stuff sacks or packing cubes** to organize gear
- **Keep similar items together** (all clothes, all toiletries)
- **Pack items you'll need first on top**
- **Keep rain gear easily accessible**

### Weight Management
- **Pack only what you need** - avoid overpacking
- **Share weight with patrol members** when possible
- **Choose lightweight, multi-purpose items**
- **Consider leaving cotton clothes at home** (poor performance when wet)

### Waterproofing
- **Use trash bags or dry bags** for clothes and sleeping gear
- **Keep electronics in waterproof containers**
- **Pack extra bags** for wet or dirty items
- **Separate clean and dirty clothes**

## Gear Maintenance

### Before the Trip
- **Check all equipment** for damage or wear
- **Test electronics** and replace batteries
- **Clean and prepare gear** as needed
- **Make repairs** before heading out

### During the Trip
- **Keep gear organized** and in designated places
- **Protect gear from weather** and rough handling
- **Report damaged troop gear** immediately
- **Take care of personal equipment**

### After the Trip
- **Clean and dry all gear** before storing
- **Make needed repairs** promptly
- **Replace consumable items** (batteries, first aid supplies)
- **Store gear properly** for next use

## Special Activity Gear

### Day Hiking
- Lighter pack with essentials only
- Extra water and snacks
- Appropriate footwear
- Map and compass for route

### Water Activities
- Quick-dry clothing
- Water shoes
- Sunscreen (waterproof)
- Extra dry clothes in waterproof bag

### Service Projects
- Work clothes that can get dirty
- Work gloves
- Safety equipment as required
- Tools (if personally owned and appropriate)

## Budget-Friendly Gear Tips

### Cost-Saving Strategies
- **Start with basic, functional items** rather than premium gear
- **Look for sales and discounts** at outdoor retailers
- **Consider used gear** from other scouts or outdoor gear swaps
- **Ask for gear as gifts** for birthdays and holidays
- **Share or borrow specialized items** until you know what you prefer

### Investment Priorities
1. **Good sleeping bag** - essential for comfort and safety
2. **Quality rain gear** - keeps you dry and comfortable
3. **Proper footwear** - prevents injuries and improves enjoyment
4. **Reliable flashlight/headlamp** - critical for safety
