# Equipment & Gear Maintenance

Troop 500G camps monthly, which means our shared equipment gets frequent use. Proper care and maintenance of troop gear is everyone's responsibility and ensures equipment lasts longer and performs better.

## General Principles

### Shared Responsibility
- Gear is shared among all troop members
- Everyone is responsible for proper care and maintenance
- Report damaged equipment immediately
- Follow all maintenance procedures carefully

### Prevention is Key
- Proper use prevents most damage
- Clean equipment after each use
- Store equipment properly when dry
- Address small problems before they become big ones

## Tent Maintenance Procedures

### Importance of Proper Care
- Moisture encourages mold and mildew growth
- Once mold grows, it's extremely difficult and costly to remove
- Proper drying and cleaning prevents destructive growth
- Clean tents after EVERY campout, even if they seem dry

### Tent Cleaning Process

#### Setup and Inspection
1. **Unpack tent** from stuff sack
2. **Inventory contents** - ensure all parts present:
   - Tent body (1)
   - Rain fly (1)
   - Leg poles (2) with shock cord sections
   - Pole bag (1)
   - Tent stuff sack (1)
   - Tent stakes and bag
   - Footprint (1)

#### Cleaning Steps
3. **Remove debris**: Shake out mud, grass, leaves, and dirt
4. **Setup tent**: Just like during campouts (turn right-side-in if needed)
5. **Leave rain fly OFF**: Allow air flow through tent
6. **Unzip all zippers**: Promote air circulation

#### For Muddy Tents
- Place tent in direct sunlight or light breeze until mud dries
- Gently shake or sweep out dried mud and debris
- Wipe remaining dried mud with soap and water solution using sponge
- Allow tent to thoroughly dry

#### Drying Process
7. **Dry rain fly**: Hang over clothesline or patio chairs
   - **DO NOT** hang over fences or bushes (can snag or puncture fabric)
8. **Clean pole and stuff sacks**: Turn inside out to remove debris
9. **Air dry bags**: Hang in sun or breeze until thoroughly dry

#### Final Check and Packing
10. **Verify complete dryness**: No moisture anywhere inside or outside
11. **Don't leave overnight**: Moisture will condense on surfaces
12. **Disassemble when completely dry**: Account for all parts
13. **Pack properly**:
    - Turn pole bag right-side-in, pack poles, tie shut
    - Fold tent lengthwise in half, then half again
    - Allow trapped air to escape before rolling
    - Fold rain fly and footprint the same way
    - Place pole bag at one end
    - Roll tent, footprint, rain fly, and pole bag together slowly and tightly
    - Turn stuff sack right-side-out (check tent number is visible)
    - Insert everything into tent bag and secure

## Cooler Cleaning Procedures

### Why Clean Coolers
- Prevent mold and bacteria growth
- Maintain food safety for future use
- Extend cooler life and effectiveness

### Cleaning Process
1. **Empty completely**: Remove all contents, water, and ice
2. **Clean all surfaces**: Use warm water, dish detergent, and capful of bleach
3. **Scrub thoroughly**: Clean inside AND outside surfaces with large sponge
4. **Open drain valve**: If equipped (for food coolers)
5. **Rinse completely**: Use garden hose until completely clean
6. **Dry thoroughly**: Prop open lid, ensure ALL interior surfaces are completely dry
7. **Triple-check dryness**: Any remaining dampness will cause mold/bacteria growth

### Return Process
Return clean, dry cooler to Assistant Patrol Leader or Assistant Senior Patrol Leader.

## General Equipment Care

### Best Practices
- Handle all equipment with care
- Follow manufacturer instructions when available
- Store equipment in designated locations
- Keep equipment inventory lists updated

### Reporting Issues
- Report damaged or missing equipment immediately
- Don't attempt repairs beyond your skill level
- Ask for help if unsure about proper procedures
- Include equipment condition in the [Quartermaster's Report](#quartermasters-report) after each campout

### Storage Guidelines
- Store equipment completely dry
- Use designated storage areas in troop trailer/closet
- Organize equipment for easy access
- Maintain cleanliness in storage areas

- Regular training sessions available

### Questions and Help

## Equipment Check-Out

### Routine Campout Equipment

During regular troop campouts, equipment is managed by the **Troop Quartermaster** (youth) under the guidance of the **Equipment Coordinator** (adult). The Quartermaster oversees loading gear from the troop trailer and closet before each trip and returning it afterward.

If the Troop Quartermaster is not available for a campout, a **Patrol Quartermaster** will act in her stead. If no Patrol Quartermasters are available, one will be assigned for that campout.

- The Quartermaster assigns patrol equipment at the start of each campout
- Patrols are responsible for their assigned gear during the trip
- All gear must be cleaned and returned at the end of the campout

### Non-Routine Check-Out

**All equipment checked out outside of routine campouts must be reported to both the Troop Quartermaster (youth) and the Equipment Coordinator (adult).**

This includes but is not limited to:
- Gear borrowed for family camping trips
- Equipment used for patrol activities outside regular campouts
- Items taken home for cleaning or repair
- Supplies borrowed for service projects, merit badge work, or other events

### Check-Out Procedure

1. **Request permission** from the Troop Quartermaster and/or Equipment Coordinator before taking any troop equipment
2. **Log the check-out** — the Troop Quartermaster should record what you are taking, how many, and the expected return date
3. **Inspect the gear** at check-out and note any pre-existing damage
4. **Return on time** — bring gear back cleaned and in the same or better condition
5. **Report any damage** immediately upon return so repairs can be scheduled

### Rules for Borrowed Equipment

- Only troop members in good standing may check out equipment
- Equipment must be returned by the agreed-upon date
- Lost or damaged equipment due to negligence may require replacement at the borrower's expense
- Equipment may not be loaned to non-troop members without Equipment Coordinator approval

## Quartermaster's Report

After **every campout**, the Troop Quartermaster owes the Senior Patrol Leader (SPL) and the Equipment Coordinator a **Quartermaster's Report**. This report is a key part of the Quartermaster's leadership responsibility and keeps our equipment in top shape.

### What Goes in the Report

The Quartermaster's Report must address three questions:

#### 1. What Is Broken?
- List any equipment that is damaged, malfunctioning, or needs repair
- Include the type of damage (torn, bent, missing parts, etc.)
- Note whether the item is still usable or needs to be taken out of service
- Flag any safety concerns

#### 2. What Is Dirty?
- List any equipment that was not properly cleaned before return
- Identify who was responsible for that equipment
- Note what cleaning is still needed
- Schedule follow-up cleaning with the responsible patrol or scout

#### 3. Who Has Taken What Equipment?
- List any troop equipment that left with a scout or adult after the campout
- Include the person's name, what they took, and the reason (cleaning, repair, borrowed for personal use, etc.)
- Record expected return dates
- Follow up on any previously checked-out gear that has not yet been returned

### Delivering the Report

- The report should be completed **the day the campout ends** — an initial report of what is known is more important than a perfect report a week later
- Send digitally via **email or GroupMe** to the SPL and Equipment Coordinator
- The report can be a picture of handwritten notes — **substance matters more than format**
- Update the report later if additional issues are discovered, but get the first version out the same day
- The SPL may address equipment issues at the next Patrol Leaders' Council (PLC) meeting
- The Equipment Coordinator will track long-term equipment needs and replacements

## Pre-Trip Equipment Checks

Before every campout, the **Troop Quartermaster** is responsible for ensuring all troop equipment is present, functional, and safe. Catching problems before departure prevents surprises in the field.

The Troop Quartermaster is responsible for:
- **Coordinating with patrols** to inspect patrol equipment (patrol boxes, patrol cooking gear, patrol tents)
- **Inspecting troop-level inventory** (first aid kits, axe yard, water containers, tarps, flags, etc.)
- **Reporting issues** to the Equipment Coordinator and SPL before departure so problems can be resolved

Patrol Quartermasters (or assigned patrol members) should inspect their own patrol's gear and report results back to the Troop Quartermaster. Patrol Quartermasters should also coordinate with their patrol's **Grubmaster** for any special cooking equipment requirements (e.g., Dutch ovens, griddles, extra fuel, or specialty utensils needed for the menu).

### When to Perform Checks

- **Routine campouts:** Complete the checklist at the troop meeting before the campout (or during trailer loading)
- **Extended trips (3+ days):** Complete the checklist at least one week before departure to allow time for repairs or replacements
- **High adventure trips:** Complete the checklist two weeks before departure

### Troop Equipment Inspection Checklist

#### Tents
- [ ] All tents accounted for (check against inventory count)
- [ ] Each tent bag contains: tent body, rain fly, poles, stakes, stake bag, footprint, stuff sack
- [ ] Tent fabric free of rips, tears, or holes
- [ ] Zippers functional on tent body and rain fly
- [ ] Poles intact with shock cord in working condition
- [ ] Stakes present (minimum count per tent)
- [ ] No mold or mildew smell — if present, tent needs cleaning before use

#### Cooking Equipment
- [ ] Stoves present and functional (test ignition if possible)
- [ ] Fuel canisters — sufficient supply for planned meals
- [ ] Pots and pans — clean, handles secure, no damage
- [ ] Cooking utensils (spatulas, ladles, serving spoons) — complete set
- [ ] Wash basins (3-bucket wash system) — present and clean
- [ ] Biodegradable dish soap and sanitizer — sufficient supply
- [ ] Trash bags — adequate supply
- [ ] Lighters/matches — working, sufficient quantity

#### Patrol Boxes
- [ ] Each patrol box is stocked per the standard inventory
- [ ] Condiments and seasonings — check expiration dates
- [ ] Paper towels/napkins — adequate supply
- [ ] Aluminum foil — adequate supply
- [ ] Cleaning supplies — present and not expired
- [ ] All items organized and stored properly

#### Coolers
- [ ] Coolers clean and free of odor
- [ ] Drain plugs present and functional
- [ ] Lid seals intact
- [ ] No cracks or damage to shell

#### First Aid
- [ ] Troop first aid kit fully stocked
- [ ] Medications not expired (check dates on all items)
- [ ] Bandages, gauze, tape — adequate supply
- [ ] Antiseptic wipes/ointment — present and not expired
- [ ] Emergency blankets — present
- [ ] Gloves (nitrile) — adequate supply
- [ ] CPR mask/barrier — present
- [ ] First aid reference card — present

#### Axe Yard & Tools
- [ ] Axes — present, blades in good condition, handles secure
- [ ] Saws — present, blades sharp and undamaged
- [ ] Axe guards/sheaths — present for all bladed tools
- [ ] Sharpening stone — present
- [ ] Leather work gloves — present and in usable condition
- [ ] Ropes — adequate supply, no fraying or excessive wear

#### Water & Hydration
- [ ] Large water containers (jerry cans) — clean, no leaks, caps present
- [ ] Water purification supplies (if needed for location)

#### Shelter & Tarps
- [ ] Tarps — present, no major tears or holes
- [ ] Rope/paracord — adequate supply for tarp setup
- [ ] Ground cloths — present if needed

#### Miscellaneous
- [ ] Lanterns — functional, fuel or batteries present
- [ ] Flagpole and flags — present (if used at campsite)
- [ ] Campsite marking or boundary supplies
- [ ] Activity-specific gear (varies by trip plan)

### Actions for Issues Found

| Issue | Action |
|-------|--------|
| **Minor damage** (small tear, missing stake) | Note it, make quick repair or substitute, proceed |
| **Moderate damage** (broken pole section, malfunctioning stove) | Attempt repair; if not repairable, pull backup equipment or notify Equipment Coordinator |
| **Safety concern** (mold-contaminated tent, expired medications, cracked fuel canister) | **Remove from service immediately.** Do not use. Report to Equipment Coordinator |
| **Missing equipment** | Check the Quartermaster's Report — it may be checked out. Replace from backup or notify Equipment Coordinator |

### Post-Inspection Sign-Off

After completing the checklist, the Quartermaster should confirm with the **Senior Patrol Leader** and **Scoutmaster** that equipment is ready. Any unresolved issues must be communicated before departure so the troop can plan accordingly.

## Roles & Responsibilities

### Equipment Coordinator (Adult)

The **Equipment Coordinator** is an adult volunteer who oversees the troop's equipment program:

- Maintains the master equipment inventory
- Tracks equipment condition and replacement needs
- Works with the Quartermaster on check-out/check-in procedures
- Reviews Quartermaster's Reports and follows up on issues
- Coordinates equipment purchases and repairs with the troop committee
- Ensures proper storage of gear in the troop trailer and closet

### Troop Quartermaster (Youth)

The **Troop Quartermaster** is a youth leadership position:

- Manages day-to-day equipment operations
- Coordinates equipment loading and unloading for campouts
- Assigns patrol equipment and tracks its return
- Completes the Quartermaster's Report after each campout
- Reports to the SPL and Equipment Coordinator
- Maintains organization in the troop trailer and closet
