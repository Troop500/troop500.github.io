Our Dutch ovens and skillets cook some of the best food at camp. They can last 100 years, but only if we clean them the right way. It takes about five minutes. Here's how.

## Why is cast iron different?

Cast iron is bare metal. If it stays wet, it **rusts**. What protects it is a thin black coating called **seasoning**. Seasoning is just cooking oil that got baked onto the metal. It keeps rust away and stops food from sticking.

![A bare skillet and a seasoned skillet]({{ '/assets/img/cast-iron-seasoning.jpg' | relative_url }})
*Left: bare cast iron with no seasoning. Right: a pan that has been seasoned. Our job is to keep that dark coating on.*

So when you clean cast iron, you have two jobs:

1. Get the food off.
2. Keep the seasoning on, and get the pan **completely dry**.

## The 5 steps

### 1. Scrape out the food

![Step 1: scrape out the food]({{ '/assets/img/cast-iron-step1-scrape.jpg' | relative_url }})

Clean it soon after cooking, while it's still warm. Food comes off much more easily. Use a plastic or wooden scraper or spatula. Throw the scraps in the trash, not the fire ring.

### 2. Scrub with hot water

![Step 2: scrub with hot water]({{ '/assets/img/cast-iron-step2-scrub.jpg' | relative_url }})

Use hot water and a scrub brush or a chain-mail scrubber. **You don't need soap.** The heat in the next step does the germ-killing. If something greasy or smelly won't come off (like fish), one drop of soap is OK, but that's the exception, not the rule.

- **Don't** leave it soaking in the wash tub.
- **Don't** use steel wool. It scrubs off the seasoning.
- Stuck-on food? Put some water in the pan, warm it on the stove for a few minutes, then scrape again.

### 3. Dry it on the heat

![Step 3: dry it on the heat]({{ '/assets/img/cast-iron-step3-dry.jpg' | relative_url }})

Wipe it with a towel, then set it on the stove or coals for 2 to 3 minutes until every drop of water is gone. A towel alone leaves the metal damp, and damp metal rusts by morning.

This step also **sanitizes** the pan. Germs can't survive on metal that's too hot to touch, so the heat does the job that soap does on other dishes. That's why cast iron gets heated, not soaped.

### 4. Wipe on a thin coat of oil

![Step 4: wipe on a thin coat of oil]({{ '/assets/img/cast-iron-step4-oil.jpg' | relative_url }})

While it's still warm, put a few drops of cooking oil on a paper towel and rub it over the whole inside. Then wipe again until it looks almost dry. Too much oil gets sticky.

### 5. Store it dry

![Step 5: store it dry]({{ '/assets/img/cast-iron-step5-store.jpg' | relative_url }})

Fold a paper towel and put it inside to soak up any moisture. For a Dutch oven, set the lid on with the paper towel sticking out so air can get in. Pack it with the patrol gear.

## Stay safe

Cast iron stays hot for a **long** time and the handles get just as hot as the pan.

- Use hot pads, leather gloves, or the lid lifter. Never grab it with bare hands.
- Never pour cold water into a hot pan. It can crack.
- Tell the people around you: "Hot pan!"

## Found rust?

A little orange rust isn't the end of the world. Don't hide it! Tell your Patrol Quartermaster or an adult. It can be scrubbed off and the pan re-seasoned, but it's much easier to fix the week it happens.

## Quick checklist

- ☐ Food scraped out
- ☐ Scrubbed with hot water, no soaking
- ☐ Dried on the heat
- ☐ Thin coat of oil
- ☐ Paper towel inside, stored dry
